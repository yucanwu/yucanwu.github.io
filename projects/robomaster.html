Project overview

From late 2024 through the 2026 season, I worked on ZJUI-META's RoboMaster Hero Robot. I first redesigned and assembled a new ammunition magazine for the previous-generation platform, then took responsibility for major mechanical subsystems of the 2026 Hero turret assembly.

My work spanned CAD design, rapid prototyping, fabrication, assembly, wiring, soldering, full-robot integration, pre-competition testing, and on-site maintenance.

My role & contribution

Mechanical & hardware engineering

  • Designed and assembled major mechanical subsystems of the 2026 Hero turret.
  • Developed the 42 mm projectile feeding path, feed-to-launch interface, and six-friction-wheel launcher.
  • Designed the pitch-axis gravity compensation mechanism and camera mounting mechanisms.
  • Designed the central electronics compartment and quick-release protective enclosure.
  • Worked on cable routing, electrical connections, wiring, and soldering.

Vice Captain · 2026 season

  • Supported team recruitment and helped bring in urgently needed electrical-control members.
  • Assisted with competition communications and coordination.
  • Completed post-event inventory and return of referee-system equipment.
  • Supported mechanical work across other robot groups during development.

Hero Robot turret design & integration

I developed the turret as an integrated mechanical system around projectile transport, launch, pitch balancing, sensing, electronics packaging, and serviceability. The CAD views below document the main subsystems that I directly designed or integrated.

Prototype & fabrication

Mechanical concepts were iterated through 3D-printed prototypes before the final assemblies were produced using carbon-fiber plates, CNC-machined aluminum components, commercial hardware, and custom parts. Early prototypes helped verify geometry, packaging, interfaces, and assembly access before the competition configuration was finalized.

CAD design→3D-printed prototype→Fabrication→Robot integration

Full-robot integration & testing

After mechanical assembly, I remained involved throughout full-robot integration and debugging. Mechanical, electrical, and software subsystems were tested together, while the turret was repeatedly disassembled, adjusted, rewired, and reassembled as issues surfaced.

Scope of my contribution: my work in this phase focused on mechanical changes, hardware integration, cable routing, wiring, soldering, and physical debugging rather than RoboMaster software development.

Pre-competition development & testing

Final preparation focused on validating the Hero Robot as a complete system rather than testing subsystems in isolation. The team carried out robot-to-robot combat tests, auto-aim and launcher integration tests, electronics debugging, repeated mechanical adjustments, and cross-team development before competition.

Competition & on-site maintenance

Competition placed the robot under a different set of constraints from laboratory testing. Between matches, the Hero Robot required rapid inspection, disassembly, adjustment, and reassembly. I worked as part of the Hero mechanical and hardware team during competition preparation and on-site maintenance.

Vice Captain & team contribution

As Vice Captain for the 2026 season, I supported recruitment, competition communications, and team operations alongside my engineering work. Recruitment helped add several urgently needed electrical-control members to the next team generation.

Earlier work · 2025 Hero Robot

Before the 2026 redesign, I worked on the previous-generation Hero Robot at the end of 2024, designing and assembling a new ammunition magazine for the platform later used in the 2025 Zhejiang Regional competition.
