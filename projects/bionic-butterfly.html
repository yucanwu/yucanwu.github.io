Bio-Inspired Robotic Butterfly

A lightweight flapping-wing prototype developed through three mechanical iterations for a student design competition. I focused on overall structural design, SolidWorks modeling, fabrication, and mechanical/electronic integration from CAD to a working prototype.

Project overview

The project focused on translating a bio-inspired flapping concept into a lightweight physical prototype under competition constraints. The mechanism combined a carbon-rod frame, servo-driven front wings, passively coupled rear wings, and compact onboard control for remote testing.

My contribution

Mechanical design

Designed the overall butterfly structure and mechanical layout, and completed 3D modeling and mechanical simulation in SolidWorks.

Prototype integration

Participated directly in fabrication, assembly, and integration of the mechanical and electronic systems through successive prototype revisions.

Mechanical concept

To keep the prototype lightweight, the mechanism used actively driven front wings together with spring-coupled passive rear wings. This reduced the number of actuators while retaining coordinated four-wing motion in a compact structure.

Design emphasis

Lightweight structure · simple actuation · four-wing coordination · rapid fabrication and iteration

Design iterations

Rather than treating the first prototype as the final design, we developed the butterfly through three successive versions and repeatedly returned to physical testing between revisions.
